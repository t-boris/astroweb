## Context

REQ-003 and DEC-011 require one expanded aspect set, and plan item I-3 asks for eligible point pairs, aspect types, orb rules and applying or separating rules to be defined from the reference inventory and matched fixtures. Eight Astro-Seek captures (charts A-D at default orbs and at fixed 15 degrees) were compared. A single rule reproduces all 297 point pairs with default orbs except five, and each exception has a stated cause. Conjunction, square, opposition and trine: 7 degrees, or 10 degrees when the Sun or Moon is involved. Sextile: 4 degrees, or 6 degrees with the Sun or Moon. Semi-sextile 30, octile 45, quintile 72, tri-octile 135, bi-quintile 144 and quincunx 150: 2.5 degrees. Exceptions: Astro-Seek never aspects ASC to MC; its Fortune-to-Vertex rows are computed with Vertex at 0 degrees Aries (a reference defect: Fortune Tri Vertex in A and D reproduce only with Vertex = 0). For applying or separating, every row of a point against a planet agrees with treating ASC, MC, Vertex and Fortune as moving faster than any planet; rows pairing two such points cannot be reproduced by one rule (5 of 12 differ).

## Alternatives

1. Keep the current fixed 6 degree orb for the expanded set. It would not reproduce the reference lists.
2. Use fixed 15 degree orbs. This lists many aspects that the reference does not show by default.

## Decision

The shared calculated aspect set uses these orbs: 7 degrees (10 with Sun or Moon) for conjunction, square, opposition and trine; 4 degrees (6 with Sun or Moon) for sextile; 2.5 degrees for the six minor aspects. Eligible points are the ten bodies, the mean North Node, mean Black Moon Lilith, Chiron, Part of Fortune, Vertex, and ASC, DSC, MC and IC when the birth time is known. The South Node is shown on the wheel and in the tables but takes no part in aspects: it always mirrors the North Node, and none of the 8 Astro-Seek aspect lists contains it. Two angles never form an aspect, and each pair gets at most one aspect, the closest. Orbs are signed as actual angle minus exact angle. A point of the daily rotation (ASC, DSC, MC, IC, Vertex, Fortune) paired with a planet, node, Lilith or Chiron is applying or separating by its own numeric rate. Two such points paired with each other get no applying or separating status (null), because the reference follows no single rule for them: no assignment of speeds reproduces its 21 rows. The existing points and aspects fields keep the current fixed 6 degree orb (DEC-014).

## Reason

The table reproduces every comparable row of the reference lists, and the bounds measured with the 15 degree captures leave little room: 6.97 to 7.02 for the majors, 9.95 to 10.32 with luminaries, 2.45 to 2.54 for minors. Applying or separating for pairs of two angle-class points is not determined by the reference, and the verification report records that limit.

## Consequences

The verification report lists the exceptions in its discrepancy log: ASC to MC is never aspected by the reference, its Fortune-to-Vertex rows use Vertex at 0 degrees Aries (reference defect, excluded from the comparison), and the applying or separating status of two daily-rotation points is not shown. The parity test compares the AstroWeb set with the default-orb list of all four datasets and checks the 15 degree lists in both directions. The orb table is one constant (SHARED_ASPECT_DEFINITIONS) so a later owner decision changes it in one place.
