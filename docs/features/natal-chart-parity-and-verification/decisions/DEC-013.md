## Context

REQ-005 and DEC-008 require a cross-check of the ten standard body longitudes through a source independent of both Astro-Seek and AstroWeb's sweph/Moshier path, but name no source. The owner was asked and chose JPL Horizons (answer recorded in this conversation, 2026-11-21). The research note docs/research/2026-11-21-natal-chart-frameworks.md describes Horizons for one-off control calculations (observer table, center 500@399, quantity 31) and warns that JPL limits embedding and does not guarantee availability.

## Alternatives

1. astronomy-engine (MIT) as a new devDependency in functions/: offline and runnable on every CI build, but its accuracy (about one arcminute) is coarser than the Moshier-versus-JPL gap, and a new library needs approval.
2. Local JPL DE440s kernel read through SPICE or Skyfield: independent and offline, but needs a non-Node toolchain and a large binary kernel in the repository or CI.

## Decision

Use NASA JPL Horizons as the independent source. A committed script queries apparent geocentric ecliptic-of-date longitudes (center 500@399, observer table, quantity 31) for each fixture instant. The raw responses are committed as release evidence and the acceptance tests read those snapshots. No new dependency is added.

## Reason

JPL's numerical ephemeris is independent of Astro-Seek and of the Moshier semi-analytical model, is far more precise than Moshier, and adds no library. Committing the raw responses keeps the check deterministic and reviewable without calling JPL on every build.

## Consequences

Release evidence records the Horizons request parameters, response timestamps and the corrections used to match sweph's apparent, true-equinox-of-date geocentric longitudes. Snapshots are re-fetched only through the committed script. The one-time fetch needs network access to JPL.
